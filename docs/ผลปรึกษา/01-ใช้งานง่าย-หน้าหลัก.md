## Findings (worst first)

**1. Picking a verse in the modal fills the input and closes — the user never arrives at the passage.**
- **Severity 4** (usability catastrophe for the browse path)
- After three deliberate taps (หนังสือ → บท → ข้อ) the modal vanishes and the homepage looks unchanged except for text in a box. A non-tech-savvy reader has just declared exactly what they want; getting nothing back reads as failure, not as progress. They must now notice the ค้นหา button and press it — a fourth action nobody asked for. `pick()` at line 428 does `closeModal(); $('ref').value = text;` and stops. This is the single biggest problem in the wireframe: the browse path is the path built *for* people who can't type Thai book names, and it dead-ends on them.
- **Fix:** the final tap navigates to the passage. `ทั้งบท` and any verse number go straight to the reading page. If you want to keep the "assemble a reference" behaviour, make it an explicit, secondary choice — e.g. at verse level a second row of buttons `อ่านเลย` / `เลือกช่วงข้อ` — never the silent default.
- **Source:** [10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — #1 Visibility of system status; #7 Flexibility and efficiency of use.

**2. The typed path has no autocomplete / type-ahead.**
- **Severity 3**
- Typing is the *primary* path here, and Thai book names are among the hardest strings this audience will ever type: เฉลยธรรมบัญญัติ, ปัญญาจารย์, เอเสเคียล, เศฟันยาห์, ฮาบากุก. Thai has no autocorrect safety net in most mobile keyboards and no capitalisation cue. The syntax also demands `:` and `-` (`มัทธิว 2:1-2`), both of which sit behind a symbol-layer switch on Thai phone keyboards. Without suggestions, the most likely outcome for a first-timer is a failed query. Every mainstream Bible product solves this: YouVersion's book selector lets you "scroll or type to search" and toggle Traditional/Alphabetical ordering ([YouVersion Support: Bible.com Bible Tab](https://help.youversion.com/l/en/article/qi1gf7u75f-bible-com-bible)); Bible Gateway accepts a typed reference like `John 3:16` in its quick-search box.
- **Fix:** as soon as the user types two Thai characters, show a dropdown of matching books with chapter hints (`ยอห์น`, `1 ยอห์น`, `2 ยอห์น`, `3 ยอห์น`), and after a book is matched, suggest `ยอห์น 3`, `ยอห์น 3:16`. Accept space instead of colon (`ยอห์น 3 16`) and a plain dash or ถึง for ranges. Show the suggestion list in the wireframe — it changes the layout under the pill.
- **Source:** [Site Search Suggestions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/site-search-suggestions/); [Designing Search Suggestions](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/designing-search-suggestions/) — suggestions help users see what's available and how to spell difficult terms.

**3. No designed state for a mistyped or unrecognised reference, and "ค้นหา" over-promises.**
- **Severity 3**
- The button says ค้นหา (search), which in everyday Thai means "find me things about this word." Many first-time visitors will type ความรัก or ให้อภัย, not มัทธิว 2:1. The placeholder only describes reference syntax, so the interface promises one thing and explains another. There's no wireframe for "not found", "did you mean ยอห์น?", or "we found 42 verses containing ความรัก". For an audience with no fallback (no login, no history, no support channel), a blank failure is where they leave.
- **Fix:** decide and show it. If word search is supported, say so in the hint line under the box (`พิมพ์ชื่อหนังสือ บท ข้อ หรือพิมพ์คำที่อยากค้น`). If it isn't, rename the button to `ไปที่ข้อนี้` or `เปิดอ่าน` and design the "ไม่พบ" screen with a did-you-mean list plus a prominent `เลือกจากรายชื่อหนังสือ` escape.
- **Source:** [10 Usability Heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — #5 Error prevention, #9 Help users recognize, diagnose, and recover from errors; [Writing to Attract Readers: User-centric vs. Maker-centric Language](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-centric-language/).

**4. The 39/27-book grid has no filter, no ordering choice, and no genre grouping.**
- **Severity 3**
- 39 undifferentiated Thai names in a 4-column grid (2 on phone → ~20 rows of scrolling) is a scan-the-whole-thing task for anyone who doesn't already know where ฮาบากุก sits in the canon — i.e. exactly this audience. Canonical order is the right *default* (it matches the printed Bible, so it's a correct real-world match), but it is not enough on its own. NN/g's guidance on long lists is to chunk into related sets and not to rely on a single flat ordering; YouVersion ships both a type-to-filter field and an Alphabetical/Traditional toggle in this exact control.
- **Fix:** add a small filter field at the top of the modal (`พิมพ์เพื่อค้นชื่อหนังสือ`) that narrows the grid live, plus lightweight section headings inside the OT tab (กฎหมาย / ประวัติศาสตร์ / บทกวี / ผู้เผยพระวจนะ) and the NT tab (พระกิตติคุณ / กิจการ / จดหมาย / วิวรณ์) — worded plainly, e.g. `เรื่องราวชีวิตพระเยซู` rather than `พระกิตติคุณ` alone.
- **Sources:** [How Chunking Helps Content Processing](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/chunking/); [Alphabetical Sorting Must (Mostly) Die](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/alphabetical-sorting-must-mostly-die/); [YouVersion Support: Bible.com Bible Tab](https://help.youversion.com/l/en/article/qi1gf7u75f-bible-com-bible).

**5. No "continue reading" / recently-read shortcut anywhere on the homepage.**
- **Severity 3**
- With no login, the site still has localStorage. The single highest-frequency homepage job for a returning reader is "put me back where I was yesterday", and right now that costs a full re-type or a full three-step browse every time. Every mainstream Bible app surfaces recents/history prominently (YouVersion has [Bible Reading History](https://help.youversion.com/l/en/article/phc6srbyii-bible-reading-history) and shows recents in the book selector).
- **Fix:** under the example chips, a row like `อ่านต่อจากเดิม: ยอห์น 3` plus 2–3 recent references as chips. Show nothing on a first visit — that's when the examples do their job.
- **Source:** [10 Usability Heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — #6 Recognition rather than recall, #7 Flexibility and efficiency.

**6. The placeholder carries the only instruction, and it disappears the moment typing starts (and truncates before that).**
- **Severity 3**
- `พิมพ์ชื่อหนังสือ บท ข้อ เช่น มัทธิว 2:1-2` is ~40 characters. In the desktop pill (620px max, minus a ~145px เลือกหนังสือ button and a ~100px ค้นหา button) it has roughly 340px to live in — borderline. In the 320px mobile frame it has ~170px and will clip to about `พิมพ์ชื่อหนังสือ บท ข…`, hiding the example that does the actual teaching. Worse, the syntax rule vanishes at the first keystroke, exactly when the user needs it. NN/g accepts placeholders in a simple one-field search box, but not as the sole carrier of instructions users need *while* typing.
- **Fix:** short placeholder (`เช่น มัทธิว 2:1-2`) plus a persistent grey hint line below the pill (`พิมพ์ชื่อหนังสือ แล้วตามด้วยบทและข้อ`) that never disappears. Show that hint line in the wireframe so it's budgeted for.
- **Source:** [Placeholders in Form Fields Are Harmful](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-placeholders/).

**7. "พันธสัญญาเดิม / พันธสัญญาใหม่" is church jargon presented as the first decision in the modal.**
- **Severity 3**
- These are the very first two words a browsing user must choose between, and for a Thai person who has never been to church, "พันธสัญญา" is close to meaningless — it doesn't map onto anything in daily language. This is the interface's front door for the non-typing user and it's gated on insider vocabulary. (The tabs also default to OT — see #10 — so a first-timer looking for ยอห์น is sent to the wrong half by default.)
- **Fix:** keep the standard terms (they're what the printed Bible uses) but add a plain second line inside each tab or immediately under them — `พันธสัญญาเดิม · ก่อนพระเยซู` / `พันธสัญญาใหม่ · เรื่องพระเยซูและคริสตจักรยุคแรก`. Verify the wording with 3–5 non-churchgoers; don't guess.
- **Source:** [10 Usability Heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — #2 Match between the system and the real world; [UX Writing: FAQs from Practitioners](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ux-writing-faqs/) — lead with plain language, don't assume untested jargon lands.

**8. Desktop reads the two paths as one compound widget with a scope filter; mobile reads them as two tools.**
- **Severity 2**
- Putting `เลือกหนังสือ` inside the pill, on the left, with a divider, is visually identical to the scope dropdown pattern (Amazon's "All Departments"). NN/g's finding on that pattern is that users read it as *narrowing the search*, not as an alternative route — so a plausible misreading here is "this restricts my search to one book." On mobile the same control becomes a full-width button below the pill, which correctly reads as an alternative. Same control, two mental models, and (line 246 vs 265) two different labels: `เลือกหนังสือ` vs `เลือกหนังสือ บท ข้อ`.
- **Fix:** pull it out of the pill on desktop too. Under the search box: `หรือ` then a single secondary button `เลือกหนังสือ บท ข้อ จากรายชื่อ` — one label everywhere, on both screens. This also frees the pill's full width for the placeholder (see #6) and for the suggestion dropdown (#2).
- **Sources:** [Scoped Search: Dangerous, but Sometimes Useful](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scoped-search/); [10 Usability Heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — #4 Consistency and standards.

**9. The two paths can't produce the same thing: chips advertise ranges, the modal can't build one.**
- **Severity 2**
- `สุภาษิต 3:5-6` sits on the homepage as an example of what the site understands, but the modal only ever emits `book chapter` (ทั้งบท) or `book chapter:verse`. A user who learns "ranges are a thing" from the chip and then goes to the modal to build one will not find it. Inconsistency between two routes to the same goal.
- **Fix:** either let the verse step take a second tap to set the end verse (first tap = start, hint changes to `แตะข้อสุดท้าย หรือกด อ่านเลย`), or drop range syntax from the homepage examples and let it be a power-user feature discovered on the reading page.
- **Source:** [10 Usability Heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — #4 Consistency and standards.

**10. The modal always resets to พันธสัญญาเดิม and forgets everything else.**
- **Severity 2**
- `openModal()` hard-sets `testament = 'OT'` (line 436). The passages a Thai general-public site will actually get asked for — ยอห์น 3:16, มัทธิว, สดุดี 23 — are split, but two of the three homepage examples are NT/Psalms, and a newcomer to Christianity is almost always pointed at the Gospels first. Every open costs an extra tap, forever, with no memory of the last book or tab.
- **Fix:** remember the last tab and last book for the session (or in localStorage); on a true first visit, consider defaulting to พันธสัญญาใหม่ given who this site is for, or show a small `หนังสือที่คนเปิดบ่อย` row above the tabs (ยอห์น, มัทธิว, สดุดี, ปฐมกาล, สุภาษิต).
- **Source:** [10 Usability Heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — #6 Recognition rather than recall.

**11. There is no way back to the reading tool from anywhere, and the wordmark is not a link.**
- **Severity 2**
- `<span class="brand">พระคำ.ชีวิต</span>` (line 217) is inert, and the nav has no home / read item. Once a visitor taps เพลง or แหล่งข้อมูล, the standard web escape hatch (click the logo) doesn't exist, and there's no nav label pointing back to the site's one real job. For a low-confidence user, that's a trap.
- **Fix:** make the wordmark a link to `/`, and add `อ่านพระคัมภีร์` as the first nav item on both desktop and in the drawer.
- **Source:** [Top 10 Guidelines for Homepage Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/top-ten-guidelines-for-homepage-usability/); [10 Usability Heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — #3 User control and freedom.

**12. Three of five nav labels are vague or ambiguous to a first-time visitor.**
- **Severity 2**
- `เล่าประสบการณ์` is a verb phrase — it invites *me* to submit a story, but it probably leads to *other people's* stories. `ความรู้ประกอบ` is abstract: knowledge accompanying what, in what form? `แหล่งข้อมูล` is the classic maker-centric catch-all ("Resources") that means nothing until clicked, and it overlaps semantically with `ความรู้ประกอบ` — two labels a user can't choose between is worse than one. `เพลง` and `เกี่ยวกับเรา` are clear and correctly placed (about-us last).
- **Fix:** noun phrases that name the content: `เรื่องเล่าจากผู้อ่าน`, `บทความอธิบายพระคัมภีร์`, and either merge แหล่งข้อมูล into the articles section or name what's in it (`ดาวน์โหลด/ลิงก์ที่เกี่ยวข้อง`). Order: `อ่านพระคัมภีร์ · บทความอธิบาย · เรื่องเล่าจากผู้อ่าน · เพลง · เกี่ยวกับเรา`.
- **Source:** [Writing to Attract Readers: User-centric vs. Maker-centric Language](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-centric-language/); [Microcontent: How to Write Headlines, Page Titles, and Subject Lines](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/microcontent-how-to-write-headlines-page-titles-and-subject-lines/).

**13. The tagline's middle clause leads with a scholar feature.**
- **Severity 2**
- `อ่านพระคัมภีร์ไทย เทียบคำต้นฉบับได้ทุกคำ ใช้ฟรี` — clause 1 and clause 3 are excellent (what it is, and free, both in plain Thai). But `เทียบคำต้นฉบับได้ทุกคำ` assumes the reader knows there *is* an original-language text, that it's Hebrew/Greek, and that word-level comparison is something they'd want. For a general-public first-timer that's the least relevant of the three claims, sitting in the most prominent middle position. NN/g's tagline guidance is that it must say what the site does, simply and scannably.
- **Fix:** lead with the everyday benefit and make the differentiator concrete: `อ่านพระคัมภีร์ภาษาไทยได้ฟรี ไม่ต้องสมัคร · แตะคำไหนก็ดูความหมายจากต้นฉบับได้`. "ไม่ต้องสมัคร" is a real, valuable, instantly understood claim that the current tagline omits.
- **Source:** [Tagline Blues: What's the Site About?](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tagline-blues-whats-the-site-about/); [Homepage Design: 5 Fundamental Principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/).

**14. The example chips only fill the box, and the caption doesn't set that expectation.**
- **Severity 2**
- Fill-don't-submit is a legitimate choice — NN/g's guidance on suggestion pills explicitly allows either "trigger directly" or "insert into the input for the user to edit". The problem is that nothing tells the user which one they got. The caption `ลองกดตัวอย่าง` promises an action; after tapping, the chip text lands in the box, the modal-less page looks otherwise unchanged, and a low-confidence user waits. The chips themselves are genuinely well chosen — three famous passages that also happen to teach the three input shapes (verse / whole chapter / range) — and centring them right under the box is the right place.
- **Fix:** simplest is to make them submit and go — they're examples of *results*, not of *typing*. If they must stay fill-only, change the caption to `แตะเพื่อใส่ในช่อง แล้วกดค้นหา` and visibly draw attention to the ค้นหา button after a chip is tapped.
- **Source:** [Prompt Suggestions](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/prompt-suggestions/) — pills should either trigger the prompt directly or insert an editable prompt.

**15. Breadcrumb chevron and label don't quite describe where each crumb goes.**
- **Severity 1**
- `‹ หนังสือ › ปฐมกาล › บทที่ 3`: the leading `‹` marks the first crumb as "back", but the trail is really a path, and at verse level it's the *middle* crumb (the book name) that returns you to the chapter list — a user wanting "different chapter, same book" has to guess that tapping ปฐมกาล does it, since the word says "book". Also `บทที่ 3` is plain text while the others are buttons, which is correct trail behaviour and fine.
- **Fix:** drop the `‹` and label the crumbs by destination: `หนังสือทั้งหมด › ปฐมกาล (เลือกบท) › บทที่ 3`. Or keep the trail and add a plain `‹ ย้อนกลับ` button in the modal header.
- **Source:** [10 Usability Heuristics](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/) — #2 Match between the system and the real world, #6 Recognition rather than recall.

**16. The wordmark appears twice above the fold.**
- **Severity 1**
- `พระคำ.ชีวิต` in the header and again at 2.4rem in the hero. On the 320px frame that pushes the search box — the one thing the page exists for — further down. Homepage guidance is to identify the site once, in the top-left, and spend the hero space on purpose and action.
- **Fix:** shrink or drop the hero wordmark and let the tagline plus the search box own the hero.
- **Source:** [Top 10 Guidelines for Homepage Usability](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/top-ten-guidelines-for-homepage-usability/).

## สิ่งที่ทำได้ดีแล้ว

- **Centre-stage search is the right primary action.** The site has exactly one job, and the homepage says so with its layout. Don't let round 2 or 3 demote it for a carousel, a verse-of-the-day block, or promotional content — those belong below the box, if at all.
- **The dual path itself is correct.** Offering both "type it" and "browse it" is right for a wide-age, mixed-confidence audience; the fix in #8 is about how they're *presented*, not about removing either one.
- **Canonical book order in the modal.** It matches the printed Bible people have on their shelf — the correct default. Add a filter and grouping on top of it; don't replace it with alphabetical.
- **The chapter and verse number grids.** A tap-target grid of numbers is exactly what mainstream Bible apps do, and it's the right call here. Just make sure the design survives สดุดี 119 (176 verses) and สดุดี (150 chapters) without breaking the modal.
- **`ทั้งบท` placed first in the verse grid.** Genuinely good — whole-chapter is the most common intent, and putting it before verse 1 saves the majority case a decision. Keep it.
- **The three example chips.** Well chosen (famous, span OT/NT, teach three input formats) and well placed (centred, immediately under the box). Only the click behaviour needs a decision.
- **Placeholder includes a concrete example, not just an instruction.** `เช่น มัทธิว 2:1-2` teaches the format by showing it. Keep the example; just shorten the surrounding words and move the instruction to a persistent line.
- **`ใช้ฟรี` in the tagline.** For this audience that's a real objection removed in two syllables. Keep it and add `ไม่ต้องสมัคร`.
- **`เพลง` and `เกี่ยวกับเรา`.** Plain, unambiguous, and about-us correctly sits last.
- **Modal opens focus on the first tab, not the close button** (line 441, with the comment explaining why). That's a deliberate, correct choice for thumb users — don't let a later refactor undo it.
- **Escape closes the modal first, then the drawer** (lines 468–472). Correct precedence.
- **The `พันธสัญญาเดิม / พันธสัญญาใหม่` split itself.** Two tabs is the right granularity — it halves the list without inventing categories. Only the wording needs help (#7).
